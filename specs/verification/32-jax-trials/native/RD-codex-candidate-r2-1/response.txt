The two domain references are `references/scientific.md` for irregular continuous-time models and `references/geometric.md` for equivariant 3D outputs. Read `references/frameworks.md` alongside either one: it explains why these tasks use native framework paths even though new general neural training defaults to Flax NNX + Optax + Orbax.

| Task | Local reference | Framework choice and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Irregular continuous-time observations, neural ODEs/CDEs, and solver-dependent gradients | [scientific.md](/private/tmp/jax-skill-trials-pAYZ0t/guidance-candidate-r2-deep-learning/references/scientific.md) | **Equinox + Diffrax + Optax.** Equinox supplies model PyTrees and filtered differentiation of trainable floating-point leaves; Diffrax supplies native differential-equation terms, solvers, adaptive controllers, saved output times, and adjoints; Optax supplies optimization. This is the solver-coupled scientific path. |
| Graphs or point clouds with invariant/equivariant scalar or vector outputs in 3D | [geometric.md](/private/tmp/jax-skill-trials-pAYZ0t/guidance-candidate-r2-deep-learning/references/geometric.md) | **e3nn-jax through its documented Equinox or Flax Linen integration.** Its irreducible representations and parity-aware layers express the required transformation laws. The reference's concrete example uses Equinox; Linen is another supported integration. Ordinary graph message passing alone does not establish rotation equivariance. |

For continuous time, choose the mathematical family from the information flow: a neural ODE learns a vector field that evolves a state between observations; a neural CDE evolves a hidden state driven by an interpolated observation path, with time included as a channel. Keep each trajectory's actual observation mask and original initial time. Solver interpolation creates predictions, not measured targets. The scientific reference also specifies solver status handling, adjoint choice, and fixed-model prediction/gradient comparisons across tolerances against an analytic or finite-difference reference.

For 3D outputs, first declare the symmetry group and output action. SO(3) covers proper rotations; O(3) additionally requires inversion/reflection behavior. Specify irreps and parity, such as `0e` for an even scalar, `1o` for a polar vector, and `1e` for an axial vector. Use compatible tensor products, nonlinearities, and scalar gates, and check the actual neighbor and aggregation rules. Translation behavior requires its own contract, commonly based on relative coordinates.

[frameworks.md](/private/tmp/jax-skill-trials-pAYZ0t/guidance-candidate-r2-deep-learning/references/frameworks.md) explicitly treats NNX interoperability with e3nn as a separate compatibility question. Preserve a valid existing Linen or direct-JAX implementation unless migration serves the task; computation and differentiation remain in JAX on all these paths.

These are reference-based framework recommendations. I did not execute the examples or verify package compatibility in this session.
